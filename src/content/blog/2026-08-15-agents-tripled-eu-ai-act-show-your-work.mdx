Two things happened in the first two weeks of August that, read together, define exactly where enterprise AI is headed.

[On August 10, Salesforce published the second edition of its Agentic Enterprise Index](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/agentic-enterprise-index-insights-2026/) — drawn from aggregated usage data across thousands of Agentforce customers plus a survey of 4,689 respondents. The headline: enterprise AI agent deployments nearly tripled year-over-year. The average organization went from 5 activated agents in February 2025 to 13 by April 2026, while average creation time dropped 53%. Agent capabilities expanded from 2 skills to 6 on average, with retail agents stretching to 9 during peak periods. Employee usage tripled. Retailers using agents saw 4x higher online sales growth.

[On August 2 — eight days earlier — the EU AI Act's enforcement provisions went live.](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-starts-enforcing-ai-act-rules-and-new-transparency-requirements-2-august) Any AI-powered chatbot, voice agent, or interactive system deployed in the EU must now disclose at the start of every interaction that users are talking to AI. AI-generated content must carry machine-readable labels. Enterprises must demonstrate risk management in operation, human oversight with real intervention capability, automatic logging, and ongoing transparency to deployers. Non-compliance: up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher.

Read those together and you get the defining tension of August 2026: the deployment race is winning, and accountability just became enforced law.

## The Part of the Index Nobody's Talking About

The Salesforce numbers are real. Tripling deployment while cutting creation time in half is what a maturing category looks like, not a hype cycle. The organizations getting 4x retail sales growth aren't running pilots — they're running infrastructure. That's a meaningful shift.

But the index quietly reveals a bifurcation that matters. Consumer-facing businesses are using agents for high-volume, task-specific work. Regulated, operationally complex industries — manufacturing, financial services, public sector — are deploying agents that handle more varied, multi-step tasks crossing business functions. The implicit consequence: the organizations with the most complex deployments are exactly the ones facing the most demanding compliance requirements. And they're the ones least likely to have the measurement infrastructure to satisfy them.

IBM's research earlier this year provides the other half of this picture. Large enterprises will average over 1,600 AI agents in operation by year-end. Seventy percent say their governance isn't fit for purpose. Only 18% maintain a current inventory of what agents they're actually running.

You can nearly triple your agent count and simultaneously have no idea what those agents are doing at the task level. Salesforce's numbers confirm both trends are happening in parallel, inside the same organizations.

## What Enforcement Actually Demands

The EU AI Act transparency obligations that activated August 2 aren't a paper exercise. The European Commission's AI Office now has formal powers to request documentation, access models directly for evaluation, require corrective measures, and impose fines. This isn't "trajectory toward enforcement." It's enforcement.

What the law requires from AI agents currently in production is more operationally demanding than most enterprises realized when they approved the deployment roadmaps that tripled their counts:

**Disclosure at runtime.** Every AI interaction must identify itself as AI at the moment of contact. For enterprises running multiple customer-facing agents across support, sales, and onboarding, this isn't a single settings toggle — it's a review of every interaction surface in production.

**Logging by design.** Automatic logging isn't an audit-after-the-fact option. It's required infrastructure. Which agents interacted with which users. What actions they took. What happened next.

**Human oversight with demonstrable intervention capability.** Not the theoretical ability to intervene. Documented, operational readiness to intervene when something goes wrong — which requires knowing what's going wrong. Which requires continuous measurement.

**Transparency to deployers.** The organizations running agents need genuine visibility into how those agents behave. Opacity isn't a deployment posture anymore; it's a compliance gap with a fine attached.

## The Collision

Here's the structural problem. The organizational posture that enables tripling agent deployments in fourteen months is precisely the posture that creates EU compliance exposure.

Moving fast means deploying before measurement infrastructure is in place. Cutting creation time by 53% is only sustainable if the governance layer scales proportionally — and there's no evidence in Salesforce's numbers that it has. Every new agent deployed from August 2 forward is subject to full enforcement requirements. The organizations that tripled their counts over the past year are largely deploying into a compliance environment that didn't exist when those decisions were made.

The EU Act's grace period for legacy systems — AI first deployed before August 2, 2025 has until August 2, 2027 to comply — softens the immediate timeline for older deployments. But new deployments have no runway. The gap between "we tripled our agents" and "we can demonstrate compliance for each of them" is now measured in months, not in aspirational roadmap quarters.

This creates a specific operational question most enterprises haven't answered: Which of your agents are EU-facing? Are they logging correctly? Do they disclose at the right moment? Is there actual human oversight capability, or just an org chart that claims there is?

If you can't answer those questions, the 3x deployment growth Salesforce is reporting is simultaneously a 3x expansion of your compliance surface.

## The Infrastructure That Resolves Both

The good news is that what enforcement demands and what good operations require are the same thing. Automatic logging, runtime disclosure, human oversight capability, task-level performance measurement — these aren't compliance overhead added on top of agent operations. They're what any serious agent deployment needs regardless of regulatory pressure.

[Gartner's forecast](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027) — that over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end of 2027, not because models fail but because organizations can't demonstrate value or establish adequate risk controls — points at the same gap the EU Act now requires you to close. Those projects presumably got deployed. They're being canceled because nobody can prove they're working. Enforcement doesn't change the underlying problem. It just means the organizations that didn't fix it will now have a fine in addition to a failed project.

The enterprises that understand these two pressures as a single infrastructure question — not "how do we deploy more agents" and "how do we manage compliance" as separate tracks, but "how do we build agents with accountability from the start" — are the ones positioned to benefit from everything Salesforce's tripling numbers represent.

Show your work isn't a regulatory burden. It's a product requirement that just acquired legal weight.
